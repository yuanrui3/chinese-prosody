# Stage 6 checkpoint

The author pilot is prepared for blind listening on sound-pattern beauty (音律美) and cold reading on ease of production (順口, 顺口). The two tasks use separate passages and works from the Stage 3 parallel-prose (駢文, 骈文) and period-matched comparison pilot. Listening comes first. Historical tone and rhyme categories remain in the text annotations. Timing and pronunciation for the audio analysis come from the waveforms.

## Author tasks

- **Listening.** Four anonymous A/B trials use a fixed randomized order and side assignment for this pilot. Two compare passages matched by period and approximate length. One uses a critic-attested wording revision whose textual and synthesized changes are compound. One inserts 120 ms of silence in a single clip with every other sample held fixed before applying a common gain. The recordings are experimental synthetic Mandarin from one Stage 4 voice and speed. The preprocessing approximately matches active level within each pair. Preserve stimulus, source edition, reuse rights, voice and edit details in the owner-only key. Participants choose A, B, tie or skip. The four pairs are disconnected, so conditional Bradley–Terry estimates are identifiable only within each pair when both sides have decisive votes. Ties and skips are reported separately. [Bradley and Terry, 1952](https://doi.org/10.1093/biomet/39.3-4.324)
- **Reading aloud.** Four separately masked excerpts appear one at a time. Record a 1–7 ease rating after each first read. Recording is optional and requires the author's affirmative consent. The review pipeline computes heard syllables per second, internal and between-clause gaps, hesitations, false starts, repairs and re-reads from the first take with waveform review. Model disagreements may flag an interval for review. A pronunciation variant does not by itself count as a disfluency. [Protocol, consent and scoring](../audio/STAGE6_PRODUCTION.md)

## Acoustic checks and machine proxies

**Controlled result.** A10 job `12472772` re-decoded all 12 original and pause-edited Stage 4 pairs, 24 recordings, with Charsiu at a 10 ms grid. All 12 measured boundary gaps recovered the known +120 ms shift within two decoder frames. Median absolute shift error was 5 ms. The insertion sites were selected from the Stage 4 model's gaps, so this is a controlled recovery check on favorable sites. Human perception of the pause and isolated textual changes to final tone, rhyme or length are still open. Full output is `results/stage6/pause-audit.json` on Gilbreth.

**Pilot observation.** The machine comparison used 72 synthetic recordings of 12 passages in six voice/speed conditions. Across passage medians, draft discrepancy and conditional phone confidence correlated at Spearman ρ = −0.48. Their correlations with syllable-duration variability were +0.61 and −0.52, respectively. Those measures share the decoder. Their correlations with separately measured waveform seconds per written slot were −0.17 and +0.24. No 150 ms off-punctuation quiet gaps met the detection rule across 1,090 eligible gaps, so rank association with that feature was undefined. These descriptive results do not establish a measure of production ease or sound-pattern beauty. Full results and per-condition checks are in `results/stage6/proxies.json` on Gilbreth.

At the Stage 6 checkpoint, edition-linked author responses, human first takes, and independent model judgments were pending. Silent text-model intake is implemented. A comparison of audio-model stumbling cues with human review requires consented, reviewed recordings. A later study would need counterbalanced assignments, connected comparisons, repeated judgments, and independent read-aloud review.
