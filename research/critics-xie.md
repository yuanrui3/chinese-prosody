# What Xie Ji's 《四六談麈》 actually judges

**Established finding.** The complete surviving one-juan commentary by Xie Ji (謝伋) gives examples of paired prose (四六) meant for public proclamation. In the two recensions screened, no passage supplies two attested wordings and explicitly prefers one for its sound, pause pattern, rhythm, or rhyme. The richest case concerns Ding Wei (丁謂). A separate Song source explicitly prefers one word there and explains why. The explanation concerns its relation to the word across the couplet. ## Coverage and limits

The complete body was read from the opening on proclamations through 趙祖潁's Mid-Autumn invitation. The pinned 四庫 transcription has 54 body lines in its digital layout (25–78). Some combine multiple notices. The National Library witness includes additional notices, including the Wang and Liu/He examples above. The 1141 authorial preface and the later 四庫 editors' abstract were read separately from Xie's examples. An edition's omission is evidence about that witness alone. The table records comparison candidates and specific judgments close enough to the research question to need an inclusion decision. **Project hypothesis.** The Ding, Su and Wang wordings provide controlled material for asking which written changes a modern Mandarin renderer makes audible. Xie's general remarks about public delivery do not rank these variants by perceived musical beauty (音律美). This source supplies no production-fluency (顺口) observations. The complete witness texts, source revisions, public-domain scan hashes and original digital punctuation remain with the study on Gilbreth in `editions/xie/`.

**UNVERIFIED.** Independent copies of the competing drafts by Chen/Qi and Liu/He, and of the rejected alternatives to Wang Anshi, were not located. Gong's story explaining who changed 寸 to 巧 remains a report about transmission.
