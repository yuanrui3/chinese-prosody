# What the evidence supports about 顺口 and 音律美

## Established findings

**Historical form is measurable within a specified convention.** The annotated Tang anthology shows flexible tone positions in regulated verse (近体诗), and its observed syntactic patterns differ in frequency even among permissible scansions. Tang examination rhapsodies (律赋) also show recurrent rhyme-tone orderings. Tune-specific sources for lyric verse (词) and song forms (曲) distinguish rhyme and phrase slots, flexible realizations, and in some cases more specific requirements than level versus oblique (平仄). Medieval rhyme books provide historical categories (韵部), while modern pronunciations supply the sounds of particular speakers and varieties. These facts make formal analysis feasible and define its scope. [Duanmu](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0952675704000132) · [Dung](https://jas.hkbu.edu.hk/content/dam/jas-assets/publications/bulletin-of-the-jao-tsung-i-academy-of-sinology/issue-2-may-2015/10.pdf) · [《御定词谱》凡例](https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/御定詞譜_(四庫全書本)/凡例) · [Li on northern 曲](https://doi.org/10.6281/NTUCL.2016.09.54.03) · [Chen on historical tone reflexes](https://doi.org/10.1017/CBO9780511486364.003)

**Readers and listeners respond to formal cues.** In a Mandarin listening task, changes to rhyme vowels and tonal categories yielded distinct event-related potential (ERP) effects. In another experiment, listeners grouped strictly timed synthetic quatrains into lines without natural pauses. Silent-reading experiments report rhyme expectancy and tone-pattern responses, with some interactions with meaning. These findings establish sensitivity to cues under specific tasks. Ease of articulation, aesthetic preference, and recognition of a familiar pattern remain distinct outcomes. [Li, Wang, and Yang](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0085683) · [Teng et al.](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cub.2020.01.059) · [Chen et al.](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cognition.2016.05.007) · [Zhang et al., extended abstract](https://doi.org/10.3724/SP.J.1041.2026.0437)

**Performance changes the audible pattern.** The observed level-tone and phrase-final lengthening in individual chanting (吟诵) traditions can coexist with checked syllables (入声) and with different foot groupings in spoken recitation (朗诵). Citation tone, context-conditioned tone, fundamental frequency (F0), and rhythmic position supply different measurements. In a matched-melody popular song sample, 77% of Cantonese and 38% of Mandarin adjacent-note transitions followed the annotated tone direction. Those rates pertain to the sampled settings. For recorded classical Mandarin poetry, expressive delivery also shifted listeners’ emotion ratings for the same text. [Yang and Kong](https://www.internationalphoneticassociation.org/icphs-proceedings/ICPhS2011/OnlineProceedings/RegularSession/Yang%2C%20Feng/Yang%2C%20Feng.pdf) · [Cai](https://doi.org/10.12677/ml.2025.13111179) · [Xu](https://doi.org/10.1006/jpho.1996.0034) · [Kirby](https://www.phonetik.uni-muenchen.de/~jkirby/docs/kirby2023comparative.pdf) · [Li et al.](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0345072)

## Contested claims

- **A single tonal rule system for all periods or genres.** The Eight Defects (八病) survive with variant definitions, and early Tang compliance depends on period and how a rule is defined. 启功’s periodic account generates the idealized five-character forms in his examples. Moving from line forms to a poem also requires rhyme placement and linking and opposition (粘对). The coverage of these rules in older verse (古体诗), parallel prose (骈文), and individual tune families needs separate estimates. [《文镜秘府论》](https://zh.wikisource.org/zh-hant/文鏡秘府論#西卷) · [Tsai](https://doi.org/10.30407/BDCL.201906_(31).0006) · [Qigong’s oral account](https://xcb.bnu.edu.cn/xywh/jsff/14401.html)
- **Universal tone chaining in parallel prose.** Definitions differ over the status of tonal regulation, four/six-character phrasing (四六), and strict parallelism (对仗). The reviewed sources support measuring those patterns. They leave open the prevalence and historical scope of any fixed oblique–level ordering or horseshoe pattern (马蹄韵) across paired prose clauses. A classification based on pairing would also bias a test of whether pairing distinguishes the genres. [Chen Guo](https://xbshkx.cdut.edu.cn/cn/article/pdf/preview/10.3969/j.issn.1672-0539.2020.02.013.pdf) · [Mo Daocai](https://news.gxnu.edu.cn/2025/0802/c1330a319711/page.psp)
- **More conformity or more regular timing implies more pleasure.** The text corpora count formal patterns. The Chinese listening studies here mainly probe structure recognition or emotion. Regular metre and rhyme improved appreciation in one German listening experiment, but transfer to Chinese tonal verse and 骈文 is untested. Vocalic proportion, consonant-duration variability, and pairwise timing variation also depend on speaker, task, and segment inventory. A single signed rule such as “more even is better” would exceed the evidence. [Obermeier et al.](https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2013.00010) · [Arvaniti](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.wocn.2012.02.003)

## Project hypotheses

- Tone opposition, rhyme expectancy, syllable transitions, and syntactic phrasing may independently predict aspects of reading fluency. Test those predictors against human pause placement, disfluencies, and timing residuals after accounting for reader, pace, line position, text difficulty and familiarity. Mandarin tongue-twister experiments supply a controlled articulation precedent. [Kember et al.](https://doi.org/10.1177/0023830914562654)
- Historical categories and performed pronunciation may disagree at precisely the places where a text-only metric gives the wrong account of the sound. Compare matched texts across Mandarin and a checked-coda variety, with repeated readers where possible. Modern Cantonese and Min observations supply contrasts for specific varieties. [Wong and Chan](https://doi.org/10.1121/1.5021251) · [Chai and Ye](https://doi.org/10.3390/languages7010047)
- Listener preferences may favor an intelligible phrase structure with room for expressive lengthening. For matched clips, rate ease of following and pleasingness independently, and measure cold-reading disfluency as a production outcome. Professional delivery can help identify useful acoustic measures and test sensitivity to style. [Li et al.](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0345072) · [Tian and Ai](https://doi.org/10.16027/j.cnki.cn31-2043/h.2024.05.006)

## Proposed contribution

Existing tools already check formal verse rules and score readings with pronunciation and acoustic features. Multi-reader experiments also show that a poem's audible boundaries shift with the delivery. The proposed contribution is to estimate, for the same passages, which text features predict difficulty during a first reading and which predict listener preference. The distinctive test is in parallel prose, using annotated paired clauses and period-matched prose controls. [NTU’s rule documentation](https://ppas.cl.ntu.edu.tw/hanshi/TopMenu/Statistics) · [Singsound’s documentation](https://open.singsound.com/doc/engine?detail=false&type=engine-cn-cn.poet.score) · [Zhang, Feng, and Xu](https://doi.org/10.21437/SpeechProsody.2026-57)

An illustrative hypothesis is that a clause-final tone contrast helps listeners hear the two halves of a prose pair, while difficulty in the middle of a clause may be better predicted by its syntax or the sequence of sounds. Mark actual pauses, repairs, and rereadings, and relate them to text position. For listening, compare matched recordings of the same passage under different deliveries and carefully reviewed text edits read under the same delivery instructions. Test both outcomes on passages and speakers held out from fitting. A tool can then play the flagged audio and state the relevant text feature and observed event. Whether those cues have the proposed effects remains an empirical question.

### Historical criticism as evidence

Literary criticism supplies human judgments about named works, sometimes down to a specific character, phrase, or comparison. In 《默记》, 章惇 reportedly preferred the expanded pair in 《岘山亭记》 to a shorter version he found abrupt. 张端义 and 周济 identify repeated sounds in the opening of 李清照’s 《声声慢》. 沈义父 identifies a specific tone slot that he says causes difficulty in singing 《尾犯》. These are examples of a historical preference, critical praise of a sound pattern, and a reported production difficulty. Each can guide stimulus selection and suggest a measurable hypothesis. [《默记》卷下](https://www.shidianguji.com/zh/book/CZBZZ051/chapter/1kxs7vmwg6230) · [《贵耳集》](https://zh.wikisource.org/zh-hant/貴耳集) · [《介存斋论词杂著》](https://zh.wikisource.org/zh/介存齋論詞雜著) · [《乐府指迷》](https://zh.wikisource.org/zh-hant/樂府指迷#詞中去聲字最緊要)

For each judgment, preserve the critic, edition, exact text span or variant, original wording, scope, and mode of delivery. Distinguish explicit preference between variants from descriptive language such as 顿挫 and from praise of the whole work. Include negative and mixed judgments and retain disagreements. Silence about a passage leaves it unlabeled. Texts chosen because a critic praised them can develop hypotheses. Validate those hypotheses on independently selected passages and use modern audio to test whether the cited effects are audible and valued today.

### Methods to transfer from studies of English

The limerick experiments show how to edit one sound cue and check whether listeners detected it before interpreting preference. Read-aloud studies provide ways to model syllable timing after accounting for syntax and lexical factors, and to code errors separately from repairs. Studies of Shakespeare performances and English sonnets motivate comparing multiple deliveries of one text and estimating how judgments depend on presentation mode and listener. Transfer these designs and compare acoustic cues directly. Chinese tone, historical rhyme categories, and tune-specific phrasing need their own predictions. [Scheepers et al.](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0074986) · [Breen](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cognition.2018.01.014) · [Gollan and Goldrick](https://doi.org/10.1037/pag0000296) · [Miller and Carter-Ényì](https://doi.org/10.1080/23268263.2018.1438963) · [Frame et al.](https://doi.org/10.1037/aca0000673)

## Five results to carry forward

1. **Duanmu and Stiennon (2005).** The abstract reports strict compliance of about 32% of lines and 1% of poems, and relaxed compliance of 95% and 68%. The author manuscript gives different figures for specific matching rules and subsets. Replicate explicit table counts and confirm the definitions behind the headline rates before treating them as targets for 七律. [Author manuscript](https://public.websites.umich.edu/~duanmu/PingZe05.pdf)
2. **Teng et al. (2020).** Listeners tracked the line timescale of synthesized Mandarin quatrains with pauses and natural syllable-duration variation removed. This provides a design for teasing apart learned text structure and audible grouping cues. [Full paper](https://pure.mpg.de/rest/items/item_3212740_6/component/file_3212741/content)
3. **Yang and Kong (2011), with Cai (2025).** Individual chanters show measurable position- and style-dependent lengthening. Cai’s same-performer chant and recitation comparisons offer a concrete model for estimating a tone-class effect independently of line-final lengthening. [Yang and Kong](https://www.internationalphoneticassociation.org/icphs-proceedings/ICPhS2011/OnlineProceedings/RegularSession/Yang%2C%20Feng/Yang%2C%20Feng.pdf) · [Cai](https://doi.org/10.12677/ml.2025.13111179)
4. **Li et al. (2026).** A professional reciter’s delivery changed emotion perceived in the same text by Mandarin and Japanese listeners. A natural-fluency check showed high ratings across conditions. This provides an experimental example of varying performance with lexical material held fixed. [Full paper](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0345072)
5. **Dung (2015).** Of 97 surviving eight-rhyme Tang examination 賦 with a documented assignment, 66 were assigned four level and four oblique rhymes. Three forms of alternation within four-rhyme blocks occur in about 82% of those authors’ realized rhyme sequences. This is a concrete quantitative benchmark outside regulated verse. [Full paper](https://jas.hkbu.edu.hk/content/dam/jas-assets/publications/bulletin-of-the-jao-tsung-i-academy-of-sinology/issue-2-may-2015/10.pdf)

## Three biggest gaps in the reviewed evidence

1. **Direct measurement of 顺口.** The review found adjacent tasks in metrics, structural listening, oral production and emotion. It did not identify a verified Chinese cross-genre study that measures both cold-reading ease and listening preference for the same passages using reproducible annotations.
2. **Quantitative human-audio evidence for 骈文 and 赋.** Textual examples and some counted rhyme patterns are available. The reviewed evidence does not establish how independently annotated couplets, 四六 phrasing, or final-tone chains map to human pauses, breath groups, or difficulty across prose genres and periods.
3. **Rights-cleared matched recordings of delivery styles.** LibriVox supports a human multilingual pilot. The review did not establish a reusable balanced collection with multiple readers of the same edition across professional Mandarin recitation and regional chanting styles. Track-level licenses, text variants, mixed performance modes, and speaker–variety confounds matter. [LibriVox recording catalogue](https://librivox.org/three-hundred-tang-poems-volume-1-by-various/) · [Fieldwork archive report](https://www.nopss.gov.cn/n/2015/0731/c362448-27393605.html)
