# Can a repeated change of pace distinguish written 7s from 6s?

**Project hypothesis, 24 November 2026.** Several of the author's eleven explicit 7s in 《哀江南賦並序》 set up two balancing halves which each change pace at a corresponding point. Some do so in four punctuated phrases. Others repeat a grammatical hinge inside two phrases. The author supplied scores without reasons. The proposed explanation is ours. The 11 explicit 6s and 11 explicit 7s come from a disagreement-only review, with eight of the 7s from the preface. Use these 22 as discovery material. The other 269 sentences have no author label.

**Frozen written rule.** Use the complete edition sentence, including any semicolon, and count Han characters. With exactly four phrases, compare lengths in the first two with lengths in the last two. An internal semicolon, if present, must separate the halves. With exactly two phrases, look for the same hinge at the same character position in both. The fixed hinge list is 而、則、以、於、于、為、爲、無、非. Split immediately after the hinge. In either construction all four segments need at least two characters, the first and third lengths must differ by at most two, and the second and fourth by at most one. Both halves must lengthen, or both must shorten. A matched hinge identifies a potential written division. It supplies no measurement of a performed pause.

On each match, add 0.75 to the frozen context-and-grammar prototype's unrounded prediction, capped at 7. This single prespecified increment makes it possible to change the memorization decision at the existing 6.50 cutoff. Keep the old prediction elsewhere. Compare with the unchanged prototype and the constant 6. As a simpler control, give the same bonus for two balanced halves alone (two or four phrases, corresponding overall half lengths within two characters, regardless of an internal change of pace). Report coverage, including any zero counts.
