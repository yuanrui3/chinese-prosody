# Historical tone and rhyme beyond character count

**Project hypothesis.** Historical reading categories may carry information about Mandarin waveform timing beyond the number of written Han characters. This is a model-development test for sixth-century and Early Tang parallel prose (駢文, 骈文) and period-matched comparison prose (散文). Sound-pattern beauty (音律美) and first-read ease (顺口) remain separate human outcomes. Participant testing comes after computational development.

## Fixed design

- Use the nine existing matched recordings from the original male-voice round and independent-work expansion for fitting. Exclude the Northern Song critics’ probes and the cold read-aloud texts. Evaluate on fresh works and credited authors absent from both pilot inventories. Render the fresh excerpts using the same Kokoro revision, voice preset `zm_010`, and nominal speed 0.9. Keep the edition, historical categories, modern pronunciation prescriptions, and draft acoustic observations separate.
- Predict total WAV duration, with pauses and padding included. The baseline fits a constant milliseconds per Han slot. Two models each add one length-normalized historical descriptor. Tone uses the proportion of historically level (平) readings among slots with a determinate level/oblique (平仄) label. Rhyme uses the fraction of unordered pairs of determinate slots from the same passage that have the same historical rhyme-book group. This latter measure tests passage-wide concentration. It does not measure whether adjacent clause endings rhyme. The fitted rate models predict duration by multiplying by the excerpt’s character count. Use the same eligible works for the baseline and its corresponding augmented model. Weight works equally in the fit and in the mean absolute duration error.
- Use 《廣韻》 for the two primary duration comparisons and 《平水韻》 for sensitivity. Test observed reductions in held-out error against 999 seeded permutations of the descriptor across works with matching period, genre, and excerpt count, independently in development and holdout. Keep repeated excerpts from one work together. Keep length, audio, work weights, and the analysis cohort fixed. Refit on every shuffled development set. The controls shuffle passage-level descriptor values. They leave the underlying text, dictionary candidates, and WAVs intact. Apply Holm correction across testable main-tier comparisons. A constant calibration predictor is unidentifiable.
- Also run the existing Stage 5 within-clause, whole-clause, and stratified historical-tone controls on the fresh texts. Report adjacent clause-ending tone and rhyme descriptors and their draft waveform counterparts using only positions both modalities can measure. These ordering tests answer a different question from passage-wide timing. Historical categories and modern pitch or vowel-final repetitions use different units and references.
