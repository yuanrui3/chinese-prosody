# Historical preferences between readings of an unchanged passage

## Admission and scoring protocol

The target is a quoted passage of parallel prose (駢文), including a recognizably paired passage from a rhapsody (賦). A named critic or commentator must identify the occurrence, indicate a preferred pronunciation and identify an alternative. A direction given to achieve a rhyme qualifies. Label such a direction, a lexical correction, and an explicit judgment of sound quality separately. Two undifferentiated glosses, two different printed characters, an exam title without the candidate's line, and a reconstruction inferred solely by the researcher are insufficient. Record conflicting historical opinions. A declaration that both readings are acceptable adds no directional vote.

Freeze source-selected passages, labels, punctuation and reading selectors before running predictions. Use an intact paired passage as the scoring unit, with the same punctuation and no neighboring sentences for either reading. For fragmentary or unpunctuated sources the transcriber marks the arm breaks. Keep both arms together for this primary comparison. Where two independently preserved rhyming lines are available, also test them as neighboring rows, identically in each condition. Report that choice of context as a sensitivity. The unit of comparison is one occurrence. Preserve all critic directions at that occurrence and identify shared works, critics and reading disputes.

Use the Gilbreth frozen written 音律美 model, with no new weights or thresholds. Select exactly one 廣韻 candidate for the marked occurrence in each arm. Keep the remaining text, historical tiers, candidate sets and context fixed. Log the two candidate IDs and all input features that differ. Use the unrounded difference with tolerance 1e−10 for higher, tied and lower. Always giving 6 ties every pair. Verify identical-input repeats and order reversal. Check for earlier training or scored exposure by work and wording before describing a passage as fresh. Prior catalog or source mentions alone are documented separately.

If the dictionary lacks a documented reading under the printed graph, report the coverage failure of the released scorer. An auxiliary test may use a uniquely identified entry under a sound-equivalent graph explicitly named by the critic. Preserve the original text, record the source and donor graph, and distinguish this auxiliary result from a native dictionary comparison. Never infer a missing sound by picking a high-scoring candidate. These are source-based tests of written predictions. Aesthetic judgments by listeners and first-read ease (顺口) each have a separate endpoint. Participant testing follows model development.
### Fanqie sensitivity specified before prediction

Two 文選 notes supply both fanqie (反切) spellings but the required candidates are missing under the written character. For these two cases only, the auxiliary test can project the lower speller to its unique pinned tone and rhyme when all of its entries agree. For a polyphonic lower speller, require that all dictionary entries with the exact complete cut agree on the specified tone and rhyme. This resolves 角 in 苦角. The projected candidate contains only tone, 平仄 and rhyme. This tests how the frozen written features respond at their own resolution. It supplies neither an onset nor a reconstructed whole pronunciation.
