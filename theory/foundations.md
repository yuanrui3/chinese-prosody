The framework estimates three quantities with different observations. Formal conformity measures how a witness fits a stated convention. Production ease concerns first-read hesitations, repairs, rereading, and related timing. Listening preference concerns choices among heard passages or performances. Their relations are project hypotheses. All numerical examples in this document are arithmetic fixtures unless explicitly identified as historical source text.

## 1. Text, readings, and performances

Represent a witness $W$ as $(x_{1:N},C_{1:J},\Pi,b,g)$, where $x_i$ is a text syllable slot, $C_j=[s_j,e_j]$ is a contiguous clause (句), $L_j=e_j-s_j+1$, $\Pi$ records ordered paired clauses or clause groups (联、对句), $b_i$ gives the boundary after slot $i$, and $g$ is the versioned genre and convention profile. Each slot records its original character span, normalized lookup form with a reversible mapping, position in clause and pair, lexical token span, part of speech (POS), dependency role where available, and a candidate reading set. Allow a source span or performed event to map to multiple slots when warranted. Mark editorial marks, unknown mappings and unpronounced signs explicitly. Each witness has a stable identifier, edition, editorial punctuation provenance, and rights information.

### 句读 and matching units

Use the following initial hierarchy as a reproducible punctuation segmentation. An editorial comma or enumeration delimiter marks a minor break (逗, $b=1$), and terminal punctuation or a semicolon marks a major break (句, $b=2$). A paragraph break (段, $b=3$) takes precedence when marks coincide. Slots with no boundary have $b=0$. When a source marks verse lineation, store those boundaries in a parallel tier and prefer it for identifying lines. Content inside quotations is retained with its own punctuation. A quoted passage or line break may carry editorial structure across consecutive syllables. Store original marks, inferred levels, and any annotator overrides. Store pair ends and syntactic boundaries in independent tiers. Pair decisions are based on edition and rhetorical and syntactic grouping, with uncertain pairs recorded as such. A consecutive sequence of comma-separated spans alone is insufficient to identify pairs.

**Example.** In 《岘山亭记》, segment 一置兹山之上，一投汉水之渊 as two six-syllable clauses and mark their ordered pairing. The comma marks a minor boundary after 上. The punctuation after 淵 in the full witness and the annotation that the pair ends there are independent fields. With punctuation removed, the six-syllable division is a candidate supported by syntax and parallelism and should be marked as an inferred reading. If two segmentations remain plausible, score both with their provenance and report the range. [Received text](https://zh.wikisource.org/zh-hans/峴山亭記)

### Historical and performed readings

For every $i$, store a set $H_i$ of **joint** historical analyses. A candidate records sense, 平上去入, $T_i=P$ for 平 and $T_i=Z$ for 上、去、入, rhyme-book identifiers by scheme, and Middle Chinese initial and final in a named convention. Preserve identifiers from 《广韵》 and 平水韵 independently. Store a versioned phonetic reconstruction or feature mapping when available, with source and coverage. Baxter and Sagart’s Middle Chinese column is a transcription of historical categories. Its separate Old Chinese reconstruction supplies no Middle Chinese phonetic values. Modern lexical candidates, including Mandarin pinyin and Cantonese 粤拼 (Jyutping), use a different tier. A profile declares which historical rhyme labels apply to a rule. Use 《广韵》 as the documentary reference for initial 骈文 descriptions and 平水韵 for sensitivity and designated regulated-verse comparisons. For 北曲 use the relevant 《中原音韵》 conventions and retain their editorial mappings alongside the historical 入 category. This is an operational choice about sources, including when rhyme books postdate a text. [《广韵》 record](https://tcmb.culture.tw/zh-tw/detail?id=20131219000058&indexCode=BOCH_CountryCulture_61) · [Baxter and Sagart’s table](https://sites.lsa.umich.edu/ocbaxtersagart/wp-content/uploads/sites/1415/2025/03/BaxterSagartOCbyMandarinMC2014-09-20.pdf) · [《中原音韵》卷下](https://www.shidianguji.com/zh/book/SK3469/chapter/1lm3lpuuyd0yz)

**Example.** In the month sense of 月, the consulted 《广韵》 lists 魚厥切 in 入声十月, 平水韵 groups it in 入声六月, and Baxter and Sagart transcribe $ngjwot$ with initial $ng$ and final $jwot$. Its historical binary label is $Z$. Mandarin $yuè$ and Cantonese $jyut6$ are modern lexical readings. If a reader actually says $jyut6$, the acoustic tier can record a checked syllable when audible. For a Mandarin rendition, the measured coda and surface pitch come from that take. Both takes inherit the same historical label in the text tier. [《广韵》卷五](https://zh.wikisource.org/zh-hant/廣韻_(四庫全書本)/卷5) · [平水韵](https://zh.wikisource.org/zh-hant/平水韻#入聲六月) · [Cantonese pronunciation database](https://humanum.arts.cuhk.edu.hk/Lexis/lexi-can/pho-rel.php?s2=yut)

Resolve heteronyms by sense, syntactic position, commentary, and independent annotation. Keep disputed or unattested cases explicit. For a statistic $m$, report eligible and usable counts and, where the plausible analyses differ, the range $[\min m,\max m]$ over admissible joint assignments. These are sensitivity bounds. For example, if one side of a clause pair is securely $P$ and the other allows $\{P,Z\}$, its opposition score ranges from 0 to 1. A four-way ambiguity confined to 仄 can still provide a definite binary observation. Leave unavailable estimates missing. Use a probability distribution over alternatives only with independent evidence for the weights. In particular, a choice based on the template being evaluated would systematically bias conformity upward.

For recording $r$, connect syllable slots to performed events by an alignment $A_r$. Store the reference slot ID with observed pronunciation, confidence, times, and event type, including rereads, skips, and insertions. A reader’s actual pronunciation can differ from both a modern lexicon entry and the historical analysis. The audio model uses the speaker’s variety, style, attested surface realization, and alignment confidence. Alignment disagreements remain visible in coverage and sensitivity analyses.

## 2. Templates, tonal contrast, and constraint weights

### Templates and distance to form

**Project definition.** A versioned metrical profile $g$ specifies its genre or tune, edition and rule source, historical reading and rhyme inventory, and acceptable variants. Encode it as a finite automaton $A_g=(Q,\Sigma,\delta,q_0,F)$. A syllable terminal has fields for historical tone and rhyme, and boundary terminals distinguish smaller phrases, clauses, lines, and stanzas. Each labeled edge consumes a terminal satisfying its predicate. A slot accepting all four historical tone classes (可平可仄) still consumes a syllable. An optional syllable has an epsilon bypass. A slot specifying 去 accepts that tone, or a documented alternative in a separately labeled branch. When one rhyme class must recur, the state remembers the first rhyme class in the profile's finite inventory, and later rhyme slots check it. Parallel prose (骈文) uses boundary, length, and contrast features first. It can receive distances to explicitly named optional profiles on annotated paired spans.

**Worked examples.** The 《御定词谱》 entry for 《归字谣》 records a 16-character form with phrase lengths $1,7,3,5$ and level-tone rhyme slots at ends 1, 2, and 4. A simplified automaton can set the first rhyme class to 東 and accept closures 東, 東, any class allowed in phrase 3, and 東. The detailed profile adds the edition's slot-level tone and variant information. Northern 曲 uses its own tune-card (曲牌) and rhyme system. In a schematic 曲 phrase requiring three main (正字) syllables, an attested insertion (衬字) between the first and second main slots permits both `A B C` and `A x B C` while leaving B's constraints intact. A tune-specific rule licenses the insertion. Zhou Deqing distinguishes entering-tone reassignment for rhyme from spoken realization and uses rhyme classes and exceptions from 《中原音韵》. Preserve the underlying historical reading and the profile-specific rhyme assignment on different fields. [《御定词谱》卷一](https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/御定詞譜_(四庫全書本)/卷01) · [《中原音韵》卷下](https://www.shidianguji.com/zh/book/SK3469/chapter/1lm3lpuuyd0yz) · [Li on northern tune cards](https://doi.org/10.6281/NTUCL.2016.09.54.03)

### The periodic generator for regulated verse

**Attested formal account.** In 启功’s bamboo-pole model (竹竿), let $t_i=(P,P,Z,Z)_{i\bmod 4}$ with indices starting at zero, $P$ for 平 and $Z$ for 仄. A length-$m$ window of phase $a$ is $w_m(a)=(t_a,\ldots,t_{a+m-1})$. The exact windows and commonly cited stricter representatives are as follows. The seven-character representatives can be generated by prefixing a foot of the opposite tone to the first foot of a five-character representative. For example, the phase-1 five-character representative `ZZZPP` can be prefixed with `PP` to give `PPZZZPP`. The precise exceptions and permissible variations come from the specified profile. [启功’s oral account](https://xcb.bnu.edu.cn/xywh/jsff/14401.html) · [Duanmu and Stiennon, §1](https://websites.umich.edu/~duanmu/PingZe05.pdf)

| Zero-based phase | Exact five-position window | Five-position representative | Exact seven-position window | Seven-position representative |
|---:|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | PPZZP | PPZZP | PPZZPPZ | PPZZPPZ |
| 1 | PZZPP | ZZZPP | PZZPPZZ | ZZPPPZZ |
| 2 | ZZPPZ | ZZPPZ | ZZPPZZP | ZZPPZZP |
| 3 | ZPPZZ | PPPZZ | ZPPZZPP | PPZZZPP |

Correspondence between lengths follows the last five positions of the seven-character representative. Prefixing shifts the periodic phase by two, so the row index always refers to the window's own first character.

A level-rhyme regulated verse (近体诗) profile restricts the order of line types in addition to rhyme and length. For instance, the second-position types of four successive lines can be $P,Z,Z,P$. Opposition within a couplet (对) is inequality on its selected metrical positions. Linking across couplets (粘) is equality on the designated linking positions. In `PPZZPPZ / ZZPPZZP / ZZPPPZZ / PPZZZPP`, even-position vectors are `PZP / ZPZ / ZPZ / PZP`, so both within-couplet contrasts and the middle link hold. Lines 2 and 4 end $P$ and must separately have compatible designated rhymes. First-line rhyme has its own allowed branch. An independently best-scanning line and the same line in a jointly assigned poem can have different scores. Keep both views and a published-comparison profile with its exact positional masks and named table denominator. [Duanmu and Stiennon, §§1–3](https://websites.umich.edu/~duanmu/PingZe05.pdf)

### Weighted edit distance

For a fixed candidate profile, calculate a minimum alignment cost between the observed terminal sequence and an accepting path. Matching licensed labels costs zero. A tone substitution costs the declared weight of the violated slot, which can depend on the ordered pair of expected and observed values. Missing required syllables and extra observed syllables have positive gap costs. Authorized epsilon paths cost zero. For a single path of length $m$, ordinary dynamic programming uses

$$D_{ij}=\min(D_{i-1,j}+c_{\mathrm{extra}},D_{i,j-1}+c_{\mathrm{missing}},D_{i-1,j-1}+c_{\mathrm{match}}(i,j)).$$

Initialize edge values by cumulative gap costs. A weighted automaton uses states in place of path indices. Reject alignments that cross a structural anchor or attach one required slot to multiple observed syllables. Structural boundaries anchor the syllable alignment in the default score. A separate boundary-aware comparison requires explicit costs for missed or extra boundaries.

Report the raw lowest cost and the exact cost locations. For a matched path with $m$ syllable slots and $n$ observed syllables, also report $D/\max(n,m)$, in cost units per syllable. Resolve ties among minimum-cost paths using a fixed order and report other tied paths. With uncertain historically admissible readings, report the minimum and maximum distances over coherent reading assignments, with the best accepting path computed separately for each assignment, under a fixed tie policy. An all-unknown position contributes uncertainty. If no finite defensible candidate set is available, mark the affected score unavailable or report only the supported portion and coverage.

**Worked example.** For strict `PPZZP` against `PZZZP`, give the mismatch at position 2 cost 2 and each gap cost 3. The minimum cost is 2, concentrated at position 2, so the normalized cost is $2/5=0.4$. If the historical reading at position 2 allows $P$ or $Z$, the interval is $[0,0.4]$. The scoring profile is fixed before comparison. Initialize with documented rules or transparent unit costs, then estimate weights on training data when the design identifies them. Costs describe grammatical distance within a profile.

For learned substitution costs, include a violation indicator for each supported slot class and expected-to-observed change in the weighted model below. Pool slots when data are sparse. The matching cost is the sum of their fitted weights for the proposed transition. Learn insertion and deletion costs only from data with informative length or alignment contrasts. Otherwise use stated positive costs and vary them in sensitivity analyses. A cross-line restriction belongs in the automaton state or in a separately reported whole-poem penalty.

### Alternation and tonal contrast

Let $X_i=+1$ for $P$ and $-1$ for $Z$. Make distinct sequences for adjacent syllables within each $C_j$, disyllabic-foot heads at positions $2,4,6,\ldots\leq L_j$, and successive clause endings. A semantic foot segmentation can be annotated independently as a follow-up. At each level define contrast rate $A=\#\{(i,i+1):X_i\ne X_{i+1}\}/N_{\mathrm{eligible}}$, with eligible transitions confined to the predefined unit. Compare the ending classes $u_k,v_k$ of the two clauses in annotated pair $k$. Report both $\operatorname{mean}_k\mathbf 1[u_k\ne v_k]$ and $\operatorname{mean}_k\mathbf 1[(u_k,v_k)=(Z,P)]$. Use known transitions and their coverage, without bridging an unknown tone. Extend these definitions to the supported historical reading alternatives if a result is ambiguous.

**Worked example.** The paired clauses 落霞与孤鹜齐飞 and 秋水共长天一色, taking the 鹜 witness of 《滕王阁序》, end $P,Z$ under the historical reading with 色 in 入声. Their clause-final opposition is 1 and the ordered $Z,P$ indicator is 0. With the fixed head positions, the first sequence 霞、孤、齐 is $PPP$, whose alternation rate is 0. The second 水、长、一 is $ZPZ$, whose rate is 1 under the ‘long’ reading of 长. These values localize different properties within a work 莫道才 praised for its cadence. [《滕王阁序》](https://zh.wikisource.org/zh-hans/滕王閣序) · [莫道才 on reciting the work](https://news.gxnu.edu.cn/2025/0802/c1330a319711/page.psp)

In 《岘山亭记》 the short pair ends 山、水 ($P,Z$) and the expanded pair ends 上、渊 ($Z,P$). Both pairs have opposition score 1. The ordered $Z,P$ criterion is 0 for the short pair and 1 for the expanded pair. The two metrics therefore make different predictions, and the two versions also differ in length and wording. [《默记》卷下](https://www.shidianguji.com/zh/book/CZBZZ051/chapter/1kxs7vmwg6230) · [Baxter and Sagart, entries for 山、水、上](https://sites.lsa.umich.edu/ocbaxtersagart/wp-content/uploads/sites/1415/2025/03/BaxterSagartOCbyMandarinMC2014-09-20.pdf) · [《广韵》卷二, 先韵](https://zh.wikisource.org/zh-hant/廣韻_(四庫全書本)/卷2)

**Contested scope and project hypothesis.** Use a period-four fit as one operationalization of a horseshoe pattern (马蹄韵). In a series of independently annotated adjacent pairs, test $u_k\ne v_k$ and $v_k=u_{k+1}$, or fit the period-four cadence `ZPPZZPPZ…` with its phase aligned to the pair boundaries. Compare with fixed $Z,P$ pairs and with the reversed orientation or unstructured controls. The modern 《联律通则》 gives an ordered ending rule for 对联. A multiclause 对联 can furnish two parallel streams of internal clause endings. Test the period separately in each half when that profile is used. These constructions describe different orders of comparison. Their relevance to 骈文 is an empirical question. For example, `(Z,P)(P,Z)(Z,P)(P,Z)` has perfect opposition and chaining, while a fixed $Z,P$ orientation occurs in only half of its pairs. [中国楹联学会《联律通则》, article 5 reprint](https://www.meipian.cn/5n4u44m7) · [Chen Guo on the scope of parallel prose](https://xbshkx.cdut.edu.cn/cn/article/pdf/preview/10.3969/j.issn.1672-0539.2020.02.013.pdf)

A run is a maximal stretch of equal labels in a fully observed sequence. Report its length distribution, maximum, and any predeclared excess $\sum_r\max(0,\ell_r-k)$. A gap censors runs that meet it. For a fully observed sequence with mean $\bar X$ and positive variation, define lag-$k$ autocorrelation $\rho_k=\sum_{i=1}^{n-k}(X_i-\bar X)(X_{i+k}-\bar X)/\sum_{i=1}^{n}(X_i-\bar X)^2$ for $1\leq k<n$. Define marginal entropy $H=-\sum_{a\in\{P,Z\}}p_a\log_2 p_a$ and conditional entropy $H(X_{i+1}\mid X_i)=-\sum_{a,b}p_{ab}\log_2(p_{ab}/p_{a\cdot})$ using eligible adjacent transitions, with zero terms omitted. Report denominators and mark unestimable cases. In `ZPPZZPPZ` there are two singleton and three double runs, maximum 2, no excess beyond 2, $\rho_1=-1/8$, $\rho_2=-3/4$, marginal entropy 1 bit and conditional entropy about 0.965 bit. A strict alternating sequence can also have 1 bit marginal entropy, with zero conditional entropy.

### Estimating constraint weights

**Project model.** For context $c$ and candidate $y$, count each applicable violation in $V_j(y,c)$. Candidate families enumerate all binary tonal patterns of the observed position count for short lines, or all endpoint patterns for short stretches of paired prose. Longer spans can use local windows or finite-state inference. For phonological rules that require richer symbols, generate alternatives from readings attested in matching positions and local rhyme, lexical, and structural strata, preserving structural masks. Include the observed candidate. A baseline probability $q(y\mid c)>0$ on the chosen set represents allowed opportunities under matched positions, historical inventories, and syntax. Hold $q$ fixed during fitting and compare results under a uniform and corpus-frequency baseline.

The weighted violation cost is $E_w(y,c)=\sum_jw_jV_j(y,c)$, with $w_j\geq0$. Harmonic Grammar ranks by increasing $E_w$. MaxEnt adds

$$P_w(y\mid c)=q(y\mid c)e^{-E_w(y,c)}/\sum_{z\in\mathcal Y(c)}q(z\mid c)e^{-E_w(z,c)}.$$

Fit $w$ by minimizing the negative log likelihood of attested candidates plus $\lambda\sum_jw_j^2/2$ on training texts, selecting $\lambda$ and checking prediction on held-out works. Ambiguous attestations contribute the probability mass of their coherent candidates or a fixed independent annotation likelihood. Evaluate rule applicability, overlap, and uncertainty separately for each genre. [Hayes and Wilson](https://www.brucehayes.org/papers/HayesAndWilsonPhonotactics2008.pdf)

Potential features include a narrow isolated level-tone violation (孤平 or 犯孤) for the specified regulated profile and the independent final triple-level sequence (三平尾). Qigong's five-position definition flags `ZPZZP` when `PPZZP` is the relevant type. In a seven-character line, use the same test on positions 3–7 when the specified representative has the tail `PPZZP`. Thus `ZZZPZZP` triggers the narrow variant. A broader definition counts solitary $P$ anywhere before the rhyme and warrants a separately named feature. Keep alternative definitions in alternative model specifications. Count linking failure (失粘) where equality was required and opposition failure (失对) where contrast was required. A line ending `PPP` contributes one 三平尾 event under the applicable profile. An observed pair of linking head tones $(P,Z)$ contributes one 失粘 event, and $(P,P)$ in a required opposition contributes one 失对 event. Avoid entering synonymous violation indicators together. Keep individual historical faults (八病) as source-specific tests of tone, rime, or initial and their structural applicability. For example, one 平头 rule compares positions 1 and 2 with the corresponding next-line positions using all four tone categories, and a documented variant treats initial 平 differently. A $P,P$ initial-slot match in two five-character lines triggers the first rule at position 1 and is exempt under the second. Treat novel prose features as separately tested hypotheses. [《文镜秘府论》西卷](https://zh.wikisource.org/zh-hant/文鏡秘府論#西卷) · [启功’s account](https://xcb.bnu.edu.cn/xywh/jsff/14401.html) · [Duanmu and Stiennon, §3.6](https://websites.umich.edu/~duanmu/PingZe05.pdf)

**Worked example.** Three candidate cadences with uniform baseline and two violation features have counts $(0,0),(1,0),(0,1)$. For $w=(\ln2,\ln4)$, their Harmonic Grammar costs are $0,\ln2,\ln4$ and their MaxEnt probabilities are $4/7,2/7,1/7$. The first feature halves the candidate's relative odds. Use separate directional features if historical $Z,P$ and $P,Z$ behave differently. Corpus weights estimate relative pattern use under specified opportunities. A finite weight expresses a soft statistical tendency. Require independent documentary support before imposing a hard exclusion. If a grammar imposes the same constraints that it is being used to estimate, remove those exclusions from the estimation candidate set or describe the weights as unidentifiable. Separate models and labels are needed to estimate ease of saying and pleasure in hearing.

## 3. Rhyme structure and textual rhythm

### Rhyme density, runs, and changes

Use separate flags for observed rhyme and genre-required sites. Let $m_i=1$ indicate an annotated observed rhyme position, including internal rhyme where supported, and $m_i=0$ an assessed non-rhyme position. Missing annotations remain unknown. For the density calculation restrict numerator and denominator to clauses with complete coverage of eligible rhyme sites. Report required-site compliance separately. For $J$ usable clauses, observed rhyme density is $D_R=\sum_i m_i/J$, in rhyme positions per clause. Report annotated coverage beside it. A clause-final character is eligible to be checked as a rhyme only under the declared mask. Density is descriptive, and its relevance varies across genres.

Let $R=(r_1,\ldots,r_k)$ be the historical group IDs at successive masked positions within a documented rhyme domain. Within that domain, the strict change count is $\sum_{j=2}^k [r_j\ne r_{j-1}]$, and maximal same-group runs give run lengths. Divide changes by the number of assessable adjacent pairs to obtain a change rate. An unresolved internal site breaks assessable adjacency and censors neighboring runs. With one known site, the observed run can have length 1 and the change rate is undefined. A permissive mode can continue a run when a later group is compatible with its anchor group under a profile-specific table of attested licenses for neighboring rhyme (邻韵通押). Anchor comparison avoids turning a chain of pairwise permissions into unrestricted equivalence.

**Example.** Five annotated rhyme positions in ten clauses give 0.5 per clause. If the groups are $(A,A,B,B,C)$, the strict run lengths are $(2,2,1)$ with two changes out of four transitions. If the relevant convention explicitly licenses $B$ with $A$ and licenses neither with $C$, the permissive run lengths are $(4,1)$. Keep the strict and licensed results because they answer different questions. Dung’s Tang examination-赋 counts give a source-specific test of rhyme order. They depend on distinguishing the prompt’s assigned groups from the writer’s realized order. [Dung 2015](https://jas.hkbu.edu.hk/content/dam/jas-assets/publications/bulletin-of-the-jao-tsung-i-academy-of-sinology/issue-2-may-2015/10.pdf)

### Historical finals and phonetic proximity

Under a named Middle Chinese reconstruction, map a final $f$ to features of its medial, nucleus, and coda. Use a normalized weighted feature distance $d(f,f')=\sum_h w_h\delta_h(f_h,f'_h)/\sum_h w_h$, where the initial weights are positive, categorical differences are 0 or 1, and any numeric scales, such as vowel height, are normalized to $[0,1]$. Record the scheme and individual feature contributions. Estimate the distance between groups as the mean of final-pair distances over their attested reconstructed final types, weighting unique types equally in the first pass. Use the token-level distance when both finals are identified. Unavailable features produce an explicit missing distance or a stated bound. The model treats reconstructed geometry as an approximation and tests sensitivity to alternative representations.

For an optional soft rhyme cost, use 0 for identical historical groups, $\lambda d$ for distinct but licensed groups, and $\lambda+(1-\lambda)d$ for other pairs with known distances, with initial $\lambda=0.25$. Keep the categorical group match and the documented license visible. A small phonetic distance alone supplies no license. For a token pair whose medial agrees, vowel agrees, and coda differs, weights $(1,2,2)$ give $d=2/5=0.4$. The licensed cost would be 0.10 and the unlicensed cost 0.55. The numbers illustrate the computation without assigning a particular reconstructed sound to a character. Historical compatibility has independent documentary evidence, for example 《广韵》 gives a same-use note on 沃 and 燭. [《广韵》卷五](https://zh.wikisource.org/zh-hant/廣韻_(四庫全書本)/卷5)

### Repeated initials and finals

For historical initials $I_i$ and lag $k$, let $S_k$ contain the usable within-clause pairs $(i,i+k)$. The recurrence rate is $\sum_{(i,j)\in S_k}[I_i=I_j]/|S_k|$. Apply the same definition to finals, with separate counts for identical characters and different characters, and preserve the position of each recurrence. For initials $(a,a,b,a)$, the adjacent recurrence rate is $1/3$ and the lag-two rate is $1/2$. A zero initial is a known category when the reconstruction warrants it. Unresolved initials are missing. The acoustic counterpart repeats the calculation on reviewed performed initials and finals, with repair incidence used to test ease. Repetition can be expressive, as the criticism of 《声声慢》 suggests. Different kinds of repetition can therefore have different relationships to first-read ease and liking. [刘勰, 声律](https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/文心雕龍/聲律) · [周济](https://zh.wikisource.org/zh/介存齋論詞雜著)

### Clause lengths and planned breaks

For clause lengths $L_1,\ldots,L_J$, report the histogram $n_\ell$, shares $n_4/J,n_6/J$, four-to-six ratio $n_4/n_6$ when defined, and combined share $(n_4+n_6)/J$. Report sample coverage, section-wise variation, and position-wise length within matched clause groups. Use the passage variance $J^{-1}\sum_j(L_j-\bar L)^2$ and the normalized pairwise variability index on adjacent positive lengths within a domain:

$$
 \operatorname{nPVI}(v)=\frac{100}{M}\sum_{(j,j+1)\,\mathrm{eligible}}
 \frac{|v_{j+1}-v_j|}{(v_{j+1}+v_j)/2},
 \qquad M=\#\text{eligible pairs}.
$$

With $(4,6,4,6)$, the four- and six-character shares are each 0.5, their ratio is 1, the variance is 1 syllable squared, and nPVI is 40. Reordering to $(4,4,6,6)$ preserves the histogram and variance and gives nPVI $40/3\approx13.3$. Each of the two 《岘山亭记》 variants separately has nPVI 0 across its two clauses, so mean length and shape must also be available. No adjacent pair yields a missing nPVI. Longer and more variable passages can have deliberate rhythmic shape. [Grabe and Low 2002](https://doi.org/10.1515/9783110197105.2.515) · [Arvaniti 2012](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.wocn.2012.02.003)

For planned breaks, the sequence $b_i$ provides candidate sites and an ordinal rank. Derive distances between successive candidate breaks in syllables at each level, treating pair ends as an independent crossing feature. For example, clauses of lengths $(4,6,4)$ with minor, major and paragraph-level ends predict breaks after syllables 4, 10 and 14. The major-or-stronger sites are 10 and 14. Neither the rank nor the intervening syllable count determines a pause in seconds. Audio tests the relationship to pause duration, breath placement, and final lengthening.

## 4. Parallelism (对仗)

Use independently identified pair spans. Tokenize each side into annotated words or phrases. A token has a syllable width, POS, syntactic role, and a sense-linked semantic category in a versioned lexicon. Examples of categories include astronomy (天文), geography (地理), seasons (时令), and artifacts (器物). For real data, document polysemy, role ambiguity, the category hierarchy and which level counts as a match. Begin with exact matching at one declared level. Phrase length similarity is $1-|L_a-L_b|/\max(L_a,L_b)$ for nonempty sides.

For an ordered global alignment of token sequences $u_{1:m}$ and $v_{1:n}$, let
$$
d_{ij}=w_{\rm pos}[\mathrm{POS}_i\ne\mathrm{POS}_j]+
w_{\rm role}[\mathrm{role}_i\ne\mathrm{role}_j]+
w_{\rm sem}[\mathrm{cat}_i\ne\mathrm{cat}_j]+w_{\rm tone}(1-o_{ij}),
$$
where $o_{ij}$ is the fraction of observed opposite historical tones at aligned syllable positions within comparable tokens. For unequal-length aligned tokens, use only separately annotated syllable correspondences in this term. Use a fixed active feature set per analysis. When an active dimension is uncertain, use sensitivity over admissible annotations or withhold that combined path cost. Report coverage of each component. For analyses predicting acoustic outcomes from tonal opposition, estimate the correspondence independently or use POS and syntax only to infer an alignment, then score tone on that frozen alignment.

With symmetric gap cost $q>0$, set $D_{i,0}=iq$, $D_{0,j}=jq$, and
$$
 D_{ij}=\min\{D_{i-1,j-1}+d_{ij},\ D_{i-1,j}+q,\ D_{i,j-1}+q\}.
$$
Report path cost, gap count, token match rates by dimension, and tone-opposition rate with denominators. Record ties or alternative expert-approved paths. If an aggregate alignment cost is needed across lengths, divide by $q(m+n)$ for $m+n>0$, since the all-gap path gives this finite upper bound. Start with stated weights. Learn costs from annotator-reviewed correspondences by favoring a gold path over competing paths using held-out works. Preserve length match as its own component. This adaptation assumes an order-preserving correspondence. Inversions, ellipsis and the position of a shared allusion can require human annotation. [Needleman and Wunsch 1970](https://bytes.usc.edu/cs270/docs/needleman-wunsch-1970.pdf) · [刘勰, 丽辞](https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/文心雕龍/麗辭)

**Example.** Two schematic clauses each contain four syllables in three tokens. Both have a geographic subject and a motion predicate. Their direct objects have categories astronomy and artifacts. Every aligned syllable has opposing historical tone. With weights $(0.30,0.30,0.25,0.15)$ and $q=0.6$, the straight alignment costs 0.25 and costs less than any path with gaps. The length score is 1, POS and role are both $3/3$, category agreement is $2/3$, and tone opposition is $4/4$. The normalized path cost is $0.25/3.6\approx0.069$. The final-token mismatch is visible even in a well-matched pair.

## 5. Prosodic expectation and surprisal

Fit a smoothed n-gram model independently for historical tone and historical rhyme. Start with a binary tone bigram and test four-tone and higher-order variants with held-out prediction. Condition on profile and fixed structural position when supported, with explicitly modeled boundary symbols. For rhyme, fit first on successive annotated rhyme groups in the same domain. Also try a stream of repeat, licensed-neighbor, and change events to separate structural expectation from rare category identity. Reset contexts at declared boundaries and at unknown stretches. With additive smoothing $\alpha>0$, vocabulary $V$, and context $h$,
$$
 p(a\mid h,g)=\frac{n_g(h,a)+\alpha}{n_g(h)+\alpha |V|}.
$$
Back off to a pooled or shorter-context model when the genre sample lacks support, using held-out comparisons to choose the order and smoothing. Local surprisal is $-\log_2 p(a\mid h,g)$, with passage means over usable prediction events and the upper tail reported by position. Score tone and rhyme separately alongside a lexical model fitted on word or character content, with no lexical tokens in the prosodic streams.

**Example.** An interior $P$ context has five $Z$ and one $P$ transitions. With $\alpha=1$ and $V=\{P,Z\}$, $p(Z\mid P)=6/8=0.75$. If $p(P\mid Z)=0.5$, the two transitions in $PZP$ have mean surprisal $(0.415+1)/2=0.708$ bits. A rhyme recurrence with probability 0.25 contributes 2 bits in the rhyme stream. This is expectation under a reference sample. Novelty, textual ambiguity, deliberate markedness, and low support can all raise surprisal. Compare to length and lexical surprisal baselines before calling it a roughness predictor. Neural models are deferred unless they improve held-out prediction and retain useful local explanations. [Chen and Goodman 1996](https://aclanthology.org/P96-1041.pdf)

## 6. Acoustic measurements from actual readings

### Units, alignment, and missing observations

In alignment $A_r$ for recording $r$, each realized syllable $j$ has a waveform interval $[t^-_{rj},t^+_{rj})$ in seconds, a link $m_r(j)$ to its source syllable where known, and a performed reading $q_{rj}$ encoded in the recorded variety. The latter can include surface tone or sandhi, realized initial and final, and pronunciation alternatives if the sound is ambiguous. Retain aligned substitutions, skipped text syllables, insertions, and repeated attempts. Store pauses, inhalations, and repairs as separate events with their own intervals. Keep boundaries marked by critics and editors independently of boundaries inferred by listening. Store speaker, variety, delivery style, take, edition, and rights with the recording. Retain a reviewed alignment alongside any automatic draft.

For example, if a reader makes a false start on the second syllable of a four-syllable clause, the event sequence could map to $[1,2,2,3,4]$. Preserve the first attempt and its relationship to the successful attempt. Syllable-based summaries use the successful realization per source syllable. Clause-level timing includes the failed attempt, and the repair rubric counts it. An unresolved edition difference is flagged for review before interpreting the discrepancy as a repair. Compare two hand annotations on a stratified sample by boundary absolute error in milliseconds and event-label agreement, then report results with and without uncertain intervals. A missing measure retains its reason and denominator, with the value left missing. Syllable timestamps alone support timing, while segment timestamps are needed for vowel and consonant measures. Multiple deliveries of the same text can yield different timings, and divergent renditions can limit exact alignment. [Miller and Carter-Ényì](https://doi.org/10.1080/23268263.2018.1438963)

All numbers below are illustrations. They describe neither an observed recording nor a proposed universal cutoff.

### Duration by historical tone and performed realization

Let $d_i=t^+_i-t^-_i$ for a successful token. Summarize duration by each historical class 平、上、去、入 and the pooled 平仄 contrast. A descriptive contrast is

$$
\delta=\overline{\log d_i\mid T_i=P}-\overline{\log d_i\mid T_i=Z}.
$$

The ratio of geometric means is $e^\delta$. For 平 durations of 220 and 242 ms and 仄 durations of 200 and 220 ms, the ratio is $1.10$. To estimate an adjusted contrast use a mixed-effects log-duration model, with historical class, actual surface tone, performed coda, boundary position, local rate, and delivery style as predictors. An interaction between historical class and style tests level-tone lengthening (平长仄短) across recitation (朗诵) and chant (吟诵). Estimate rate from a surrounding window excluding the target and its adjoining gaps. Model reader and passage or text syllable as grouping factors according to the sampling design. Report support per historical-by-performed stratum. If the two classifications are effectively collinear within a variety, report the descriptive association and the limited support for independent effects. Historical 平仄 and 入声 labels are the **text counterparts**. A historical 入 syllable can have a Mandarin open final in the actual take. A checked coda requires phonetic evidence in that take. Segment type and phrase-final lengthening influence duration even when the historical class is unchanged. [Cai](https://doi.org/10.12677/ml.2025.13111179) · [Breen](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cognition.2018.01.014)

### Pauses and annotated boundaries

For each comparable pair of adjacent syllables, let $g_i=\max(0,t^-_{i+1}-t^+_i)$ after checking that any intervening speech or repair has its own annotation. Classify intervening events, including silence, breath, and filled pauses. Count uninterrupted silent gaps at or above a preregistered threshold $\tau$ as silent pauses. Gaps with uncertain source alignment are excluded, and consonant closures belong to their phones. Where an automatic aligner alone produces a candidate pause, locate it against the waveform before calling it a hesitation. Preserve continuous gap durations as a second outcome and repeat summaries at nearby threshold values.

Let $B_k$ be the set of eligible text positions at or above boundary strength $k$, and $A$ the positions with detected silent pauses. For each level compute precision $|A\cap B_k|/|A|$, recall $|A\cap B_k|/|B_k|$, and their harmonic mean when defined. Every position enters the match once at each tested level. If expected boundaries inside a continuous recording are after syllables 4, 10, and 14 and pauses are detected after 4, 7, and 14, precision and recall are both $2/3$. The missing pause at 10 and extra pause at 7 are candidates for listening review. A pause at a lower-level boundary can still count as unmatched when testing the higher level. Also model silence probability and positive duration by boundary tier, reporting pause coverage and duration by tier. Test whether adding pairing or independently annotated syntax improves prediction beyond punctuation alone. Text counterparts are editorial phrasing (句读), clause and pair ends, paragraph ends, and any exact critic-marked turns (顿挫). A location marked 文气一顿 in 《岘山亭记》 can motivate an acoustic prediction about lengthening as well as silence. Performance style and speaking rate affect how a speaker marks a boundary. [《岘山亭记》 and annotations](https://zh.wikisource.org/zh-hans/峴山亭記) · [Zhang, Feng, and Xu](https://doi.org/10.21437/SpeechProsody.2026-57)

### Timing variability at several scales

Apply the same normalized pairwise variability index (nPVI) defined for text to positive durations within a passage or continuous run. Report which duration and adjacency were used. Compute it separately on vowel runs, syllables, feet and clauses. Fit text-derived feet or obtain independent annotations before measuring their timings. For clause timing, sum realized articulation durations for a primary clause measure and report elapsed clause time including internal gaps separately. Estimate the standard deviation of foot and clause durations as a second descriptor. For 200, 300 and 250 ms the index is $29.09$. For a textual clause-length sequence of 4, 6 and 4 syllables the same formula gives $40$. These describe variation in different units. Test whether the textual pattern predicts the timing pattern across matched readings.

With vowel-run durations $V_j$ and consonant-run durations $K_j$ within a continuous speech region, define $\%V=100\sum_j V_j/(\sum_j V_j+\sum_j K_j)$ and $\Delta C=\operatorname{sd}(K_j)$ with a sample denominator. Adjacent segments of the same category form one run. Exclude pauses, breaths, and unclassified intervals, and report coverage. For vowel runs of 130, 170, and 160 ms and consonant runs of 70, 130, and 90 ms, $\%V\approx61.33\%$ and $\Delta C\approx30.55$ ms. Require enough classified intervals and at least two adjacent usable durations for nPVI. Never bridge a missing interval or a deliberately excluded boundary in a run. Text counterparts are clause and foot length sequences, historical onset and coda structure, and syllable classes such as 入声. Phonological counts describe segment structure and carry no implied duration. Compare within matched speaker, pace, and style strata because measured timing variability depends on segment composition and task. [Ramus et al.](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0010-0277(99)00058-X) · [Grabe and Low](https://doi.org/10.1515/9783110197105.2.515) · [Arvaniti](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.wocn.2012.02.003)

### Fundamental frequency, intensity, and rhyme realizations

Represent reliable voiced fundamental frequency (F0) as $y(t)=12\log_2(F_0(t)/F_{\mathrm{ref}})$, where $F_{\mathrm{ref}}$ is a robust reference for that reader and delivery style. For tone-target deviation, compare voiced samples on a common within-syllable time grid to a reference trajectory $\mu_{q,c}(t)$ matched to the independently annotated actual reading $q$ and context $c$, including adjacent tones and phrase position. Fit the reference without the evaluated material, and report root-mean-square error on available sample pairs. If $y=(0,1,3)$ and $\mu=(0,1,2)$ in semitones, the error is $\sqrt{1/3}\approx0.58$ semitones. This measures acoustic deviation from a contextual reference. Specify coverage and tracking criteria, and check octave jumps and creaky or weakly voiced intervals. Use a separate style model for chant and withhold the comparison when no matched reference exists.

For contour smoothness, first apply a fixed, documented smoothing rule and consistent physical-time grid. Average $|y_{j+1}-2y_j+y_{j-1}|/\Delta t^2$ over contiguous reliable triples within voiced regions. Report boundary pitch resets separately from within-region curvature. At 50 ms spacing, the illustrative values $0,1,3$ give $400$ semitones per second squared for the one eligible triple. This captures rapid changes in pitch slope and is sensitive to tracking quality and grid choice. Text counterparts are historical tone-transition patterns, tone opposition within pairs, and the predicted boundary hierarchy. One can test whether those features explain contour deviation or curvature after accounting for the recorded variety and style. Historical 平仄 enters as a categorical predictor. If intensity accompanies a diagnostic, report within-recording RMS level in decibels relative to a reader- or take-specific reference. At twice the reference RMS amplitude the difference is $20\log_{10}2\approx6.02$ dB. Reading intensity can also reflect microphone placement or gain. Its text counterpart is position relative to an independently annotated phrase or paired element. [Xu](https://doi.org/10.1006/jpho.1996.0034) · [Prom-on, Xu, and Thipakorn](https://doi.org/10.1121/1.3037222)

To compare historical rhyme groups (韵部) and actual rhyme realizations, retain the performed pinyin or jyutping final where applicable and optional acoustic vowel trajectories. A simple exploratory distance between two sung or read finals in one variety is $D=\lVert v_i-v_j\rVert_2+\lambda\,\mathbf1[c_i\ne c_j]$, where $v$ is a vector of speaker-normalized vowel formants sampled at matched points and $c$ is the performed coda class. For illustrative two-component vectors $(0.2,-0.4)$ and $(0.5,0)$ with the same coda, $D=0.5$. If codas differ and the prespecified illustrative penalty is $0.5$, $D=1.0$. Include separately annotated glides and moving vowels when the recording permits. The penalty and acoustic scaling require calibration on held-out rhyme judgment data before use as preference predictors. Use reliable formants and report the segment-only comparison when vowels cannot be measured. A distance provides a way to test historical rhyme runs and changes (换韵) under present pronunciations. Use listener evidence to assess whether distances track perceived rhyme.

### Breath groups and tone–melody correspondence

A breath group is a speech span between two corroborated inhalation landmarks. Use audible breaths or synchronized respiratory measurements when available and record which supports each boundary. Silence alone supports a pause label. The group length is the number of realized syllables, including spoken repeat attempts. For example, supported inhalations before syllable 1, after syllable 10, and after syllable 18 delimit groups of 10 and 8 syllables. If eligible clause ends are after 4, 10, 14, and 20, the break after 18 is unexpected under the clause-end model. Report break alignment, the median and spread of group lengths, and incomplete spans separately. Text counterparts are uninterrupted clause or pair lengths and the expected boundary tiers. Group length describes what readers did under the recording conditions. Direct comfort ratings or independent difficulty observations would be needed to infer effort. [Yang and Kong, author abstract on respiration measures](https://doi.org/10.16511/j.cnki.qhdxxb.2017.22.011)

For chant or song, annotate the main vowel-bearing note or melodic anchor $M_i$ in semitones and the corresponding performed-variety tone endpoint target $Q_i$ from a separately specified reference. Compute adjacent changes $\Delta M_i=M_{i+1}-M_i$ and $\Delta Q_i=Q_{i+1}-Q_i$, then report directional agreement on eligible transitions and an explicit tie policy. With targets $(2,5,3)$ and performed anchors $(1,4,5)$, the target directions are up then down, while melody rises twice, giving agreement $1/2$. Measure motion within a syllable separately when ornaments span several notes. Keep the rendition’s actual melody and tone reference independent to avoid measuring the same pitch trace on both sides. Text counterparts are historical tone categories, contrast patterns, and tune-specific positions where applicable. The relation between lexical tone and melody (依字行腔) is a performance-style hypothesis. Because vocal traditions differ, fit and report baselines by variety, tune and style. [Kirby](https://www.phonetik.uni-muenchen.de/~jkirby/docs/kirby2023comparative.pdf)

### Production, listening, and detection

For **production**, assign each reader one version per passage for a first encounter in the session. Balance versions and task order across readers, record pre-existing familiarity, and retain the initial take. Use ordinary reading as the first condition. Deliberate recitation (朗诵) and chanting (吟诵) provide separately labeled contrasts. Independently mark false starts, repairs, repetitions, hesitations, omissions, and pause positions. Separate repair behavior from omissions and alignment failures. Two annotators review a sample without knowing the critic’s preference or model flags. Publish rules, agreement, and adjudication. Record actual pronunciations and rely on the historical readings for text measures.

Let $E_{rq}$ be the preregistered number of marked repair events for reader $r$ and passage version $q$, with count exposure $L_q$ eligible target syllables. The production endpoint is the repair rate per 100 target syllables, $100E_{rq}/L_q$. Report clause-interior hesitations and pace as additional endpoints. Estimate a version contrast using reader and base-passage effects and a log count model with offset $\log L_q$. For the 章惇 contrast, also report absolute events per rendition because changing length changes exposure. For an illustrative 2 events over 40 syllables against 1 over 44, rates are 5.0 and 2.27 per 100. The signed rate difference in this example is −2.73 per 100 and the rate ratio is 0.45. These illustrative numbers have no inferential weight.

For **listening**, present loudness-matched audio first with the version labels hidden, order randomized, and a reader matched within each pair where feasible. Route preference separately from “did you hear a difference?” and perceived anomaly. Include identical clips in the detection task to estimate false alarms. Keep preference responses from the full randomized sample because noticing may itself be affected by the edit. A text-only comparison uses an independent assignment or a counterbalanced block with different items. Compare both same-text alternate deliveries and different-text variants read under the same instructions. Cross readers and texts where possible. Use independently reviewed plausible edits with matched meanings and syntax as far as the feature permits. A final-syllable replacement can change several phonetic or lexical properties, so record co-changes and qualify the feature attribution. A failed audible manipulation is a failed stimulus.

Let $Y_{\ell ij}=1$ when listener $\ell$ prefers clip $i$ over clip $j$. In a Bradley–Terry model,

$$
\Pr(Y_{\ell ij}=1)=\operatorname{logistic}
  (\theta_i-\theta_j+\gamma s_{\ell ij}),
$$

where $s_{\ell ij}=+1$ when $i$ is presented first and $-1$ otherwise. Set one reference worth to zero per connected comparison component. Comparisons that only connect two versions of the same passage identify a within-passage contrast. For pooled feature effects, express the differences using clip covariate differences and account for repeated listeners and base passages with effects or crossed-cluster inference. An intercept added to both clip worths cancels, so listener heterogeneity can enter through listener-specific order or feature slopes. If the task allows a no-preference response, report its frequency and label the fitted probability as conditional on a decisive choice. In a toy pair with no order correction, 12 preferences for $i$ and eight for $j$ give $\hat\theta_i-\hat\theta_j=\log(12/8)\approx0.405$, odds 1.5, and preference probability 0.60. [Turner and Firth, *Journal of Statistical Software* 48(9), 2012, pp. 1–21](https://doi.org/10.18637/jss.v048.i09)

Measure silence separately from syllable duration. Adopt Miller and Carter-Ényì’s same-text comparison across performances to distinguish recurring phrasing from individual delivery. Use Scheepers et al.’s independent anomaly task to check interventions. Use Gollan and Goldrick’s explicit error and self-correction categories while adding a separate hesitation code. ### Validation, nulls, and disconfirming outcomes

For each text metric measure a real-minus-null contrast on the same eligible pieces. Group editions and variants by work. Run three seeded nulls.

1. Shuffle syllable annotation bundles within each clause and preserve its length and boundaries. This disrupts local order and generally changes the final position. Lexical and syntactic degradation makes it a broad stress test.
2. Shuffle whole clauses within each piece and preserve their content and lengths. Assign them back to original clause slots for the order-based comparison. This destroys the order that supports pair and chaining effects. A secondary intact-pair shuffle can isolate the between-pair effect.
3. Reassign historical tone labels to eligible positions by permuting the labels within corpus strata of genre and period. This preserves empirical frequencies exactly and holds missingness fixed. Use the resulting tone-only counterfactual for diagnostics. Preserve the original pronunciation, rime, syntax, and audio.

Specify the observables each null can change. A length histogram is invariant under all three, while a clause-length sequence statistic can respond to clause shuffling. Report invariance explicitly and use designed length contrasts and known groups to test the histogram. For a directional permutation test declaring large $T$ in advance, report $p=(1+\sum_{b=1}^B\mathbf 1[T_b\ge T_{\rm obs}])/(B+1)$. Reverse the tail for a registered negative direction. Use both tails with multiplicity adjustment or a preregistered absolute statistic for an undirected test. Report the paired mean difference and a piece-level interval. For a toy test with 19 permutations and two statistics at least as large as the observed statistic, the Monte Carlo value is $3/20=0.15$. Resample pieces and account for repeated speakers or listeners for human effects, and report effect sizes with uncertainty before making claims across metrics.

Split by work family so edits, witnesses, clips, and repetitions of the same base text stay together. Hold out speakers or listeners as a separate test of generalization. Fit costs and text baselines on the development set, then compare a syntax/length/familiarity baseline with and without each historical metric. Predefine acoustic counterparts and summarize within-style paired associations, held-out incremental prediction, and the largest signed residuals with reviewed alignment. Use score differences and held-out predictive loss for continuous or binary outcomes as appropriate. A long boundary may be appreciated as expressive and a mid-clause interruption may indicate effort. Treat any monotone relation between regularity, production ease, and liking as a testable hypothesis. Remove metrics with no meaningful separation under applicable controls from scoring claims, while retaining their descriptive values and failure results.

For later known-groups tests, record independent labels for 近体诗 versus 古体诗, 律赋 versus 古赋, 骈文 versus nonparallel 散文, and canonical versus less anthologized texts within a genre. For early Tang 律诗, record dating uncertainty when comparing material before and after the period associated with 沈佺期 and 宋之问 (沈宋). A genre can differ in clause-length distribution even when clause shuffling preserves that distribution. Use passage-level splits and report genre asymmetries and failed controls. For external calibration use Duanmu and Stiennon’s named strict and relaxed body tables and exact denominators, with their abstract rates recorded separately. ## 8. Relating text to sound and combining validated components

For each nominated text measure $x$ and observed counterpart $y$, join by aligned syllable, clause, pair, or passage as appropriate. For example, a passage-level nPVI calculated on textual clause counts is paired with duration nPVI from that passage’s rendition. Document the analysis unit and define each direction before fitting. For descriptions, report a within-style rank correlation across passages, using repeated readers through reader-level summaries or a clustered model. For prediction use $y=\alpha+\beta x+\gamma^\top z+u_{\mathrm{reader}}+v_{\mathrm{work}}+\varepsilon$, or the count, binary, or two-part counterpart as required. Here $z$ measures baseline covariates such as lexical difficulty, familiarity, style, and position. Report the change in held-out loss after adding $x$, clustered intervals, agreement coverage, and the largest positive and negative residuals with reviewed audio. Set aside entire work families and speakers for evaluation. Estimate style-specific associations if the relationship changes across preparations or varieties.

**Example.** In one style, three passages have textual nPVIs $(0,20,40)$ and observed duration nPVIs $(10,32,25)$. Their Spearman correlation is 0.5. If a toy model predicts durations nPVIs $(12,22,32)$, residuals are $(-2,+10,-7)$. The second passage has the largest disagreement. Listen to the relevant clauses and check the alignment and timing rule before interpreting it. This example shows association and a way to locate disagreements. It supplies no inference from three passages.

### Optional summary after validation

Display component estimates, uncertainty, coverage, convention, and flagged positions first. Formal compliance and acoustic observations have their own units and should remain directly inspectable. Fit distinct summary models for production and preference, since a coefficient can point in different directions. Only admit components with meaningful effects under applicable controls and reliable measurement. Require evidence for the direction of a component. A U-shaped effect can use a preregistered transformation or remain a descriptive measure.

For a convenience index on endpoint $e$, calibrate transformed, direction-oriented components on training data to $z_j=(f_j-\mu_j)/\sigma_j$, with $\sigma_j>0$. Set $S_e=\sum_{j\in J_e}w_{ej}z_j$ with nonnegative weights summing to 1. Estimate them with regularization against that endpoint and assess calibration on held-out works and readers. Use the direction and transformations justified for $e$. Show the signed contribution $w_{ej}z_j$ and explanatory source locations. Decline to display the index when a required component is missing, the genre lacks validation, or coverage is outside the validated range. An unvalidated formal score may still be reported under its named historical convention. A cross-endpoint composite is deferred until there is evidence and an explicit purpose for choosing the trade-off.

**Example.** For a hypothetical calibrated endpoint, component values $(0.5,-0.25,1.0)$ and weights $(0.4,0.2,0.4)$ give contributions $(0.20,-0.05,0.40)$ and summary $0.55$ standardized units. The negative contribution is visible. These are illustration weights. Stage 2 supplies no empirical composite.
