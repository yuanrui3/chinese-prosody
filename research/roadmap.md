# Roadmap after the historical reading test

**Decision.** Keep the written 1–7 prototype frozen. Test whether the program can recover a passage’s rhyme pattern before deciding how much that pattern should contribute to its beauty estimate. Claude Fable 5.2 reviewed the project at Extra effort on 26 November 2026 and proposed this sequence. The experiment and decision rules below are prospective.

**Measured starting point.** In six fresh passages, both documented readings produced identical features and scores in isolation. In 陸機’s 《大暮賦》, supplying the neighboring endings 逝 and 勵 let the scorer recover both rhyme matches for 顏師古’s preferred reading of 貰. Its estimate fell from 5.890 to 5.831. The critic’s explanation gives a check on rhyme recognition. A listening judgment about 音律美 requires separate evidence. [Fixed-text study](../reports/critic-reading-transfer.md)

## Next Gilbreth experiment

Use rhymed passages of 賦 and period-matched prose. In texts with independently identifiable line endings, hide one ending from the program. Choose targets whose character has one unambiguous rhyme category in the pinned 《廣韻》. Give the program two possible categories, the attested one and a different category of the same historical tone. Ask which one better fits the surrounding lines. This tests prediction of a recoverable rhyme category. Editorial preference, a listener rating, and the historically correct reading of a disputed character each need their own evidence.

1. **Seal the test.** Split by whole work and author. Put works already used to develop critic cases on the development side. Freeze the edition, eligible endings, candidate pairs, window and settings before evaluating untouched works. Mask the target before inferring where a rhyme passage (韻段) begins or ends, and remove the answer from any frequency baseline. The dictionary supplies labels without new author annotations.
2. **Compare two methods on the same positions.** The current method checks exact rhyme-group matches up to two rows in either direction within the same section. Test a second method that looks for recurring rhyme positions in a local passage and can abstain outside a supported pattern. Compare exact groups first. Test any historically documented allowance for rhyming across groups (同用) as a separate change with a cited mapping. Include the most frequent unmasked category in the work, a tone-only tie, shuffled line order, and accidental matches in comparison prose. [Current implementation](../src/quantitative_editorial_context.py)
3. **Decide on untouched works.** Give each work equal weight. Score paired choices as 1 for correct, 0 for incorrect, and ½ for a tie. Count abstentions at chance in the overall comparison and report coverage and accuracy on attempted items alongside it. Advance the new rhyme measure only if its gain over the current rule has a work-clustered 95% interval above zero, and its advantage over the frequency baseline diminishes under shuffling. Set the shuffle tolerance on development works before opening the test. If too few independent works support a stable interval, publish the examples and coverage and leave the result undecided.

## When rhyme should affect the score

**Completed text check.** Across twelve held-out regulated 賦 from 《全唐文》, two-choice rhyme recovery scored 81.1% with the existing nearby-ending rule and 60.5% with the stricter recurring-pattern rule. The stricter rule scored lower in every work. The existing rule therefore carries forward to the written-score comparison below. [Masked-rhyme study](../reports/masked-rhyme-recovery.md)

On a separate written-score test, compare a training-only refit **with rhyme**, the same refit **without rhyme**, and **always giving 6**. Use paired differences in mean absolute error across held-out works and examine how sensitive the fitted rhyme coefficient is to the training works. Give the revised score a rhyme term only if it improves held-out prediction over both comparators. Otherwise keep reporting the rhyme evidence beside the frozen score. A small but exact numerical preference in an old case remains part of the record.

**Completed written-score test.** In the sixteen-work prospective comparison, error against the primary blind editorial reading was 1.332 points with neighboring rhyme, 1.318 without it and 1.425 for always giving 6. The second reading and an earlier eight-work replay also favored the refit without neighboring rhyme. The criterion for supporting a numerical contribution failed. Continue reporting neighboring rhyme as a descriptive feature. A further written comparison can test work-balanced training against the observed 5.02–6.39 forecast range. [Results and period checks](../reports/written-rhyme-ablation.md)

**Completed equal-work test.** Training each of the seven works with equal influence moved scores upward and missed the same nine editorial 7s as the released model on 96 fresh sentences. Mean error rose by 0.125 points. The highest candidate forecast went to a sentence both readers gave 2. Keep the released benchmark. A next written hypothesis is to learn which of two lines in the same work sounds more accomplished on earlier editorial grades, then check that ordering in untouched works before tuning how many lines to call 7. The current sixteen works are now exposed. [Fresh comparison and limitations](../reports/work-weighted-training.md)

**Completed same-work ranking test.** On 38 fresh comparisons across seven informative works, the pair-trained model put the 7 above the 5 or 6 in 69.0% of within-work comparisons, averaged equally over works. The released model scored 43.6% and always giving 6 scored 50.0%. The paired interval for the lead over the released model included zero. Retain the current benchmark. A future replication can reuse the frozen ranking weights on new works, with a larger author sample to improve the precision of the work-level comparison. The present sixteen works are now exposed. [Ranks, both editorial readings and controls](../reports/within-work-rank.md)

## Audio and final validation

After the text checks, test on Gilbreth whether the relevant ending contrast is audible in the actual recorded variety across voices. Use the performed syllables and alignment for the acoustic measurement. Retain historical categories for text. The pilot changed voice and text visibility together, so its ratings cannot isolate either effect. [Pilot findings](../reports/final.md#author-rating-follow-up)

The final listening study would test 音律美 across works, voices and listeners with text visibility controlled. First-read ease (顺口) retains its own cold read-aloud task. Until then, the project can validate rhyme recovery, agreement with written editorial scores, and acoustic realization separately. [Human validation protocol](followup-pilot.md)
